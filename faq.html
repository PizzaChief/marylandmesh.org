FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

New to Meshtastic? These answers cover the radio basics, getting started, Maryland settings, and what to know about privacy.

What is Meshtastic?

Meshtastic is an open-source communication system that uses low-power LoRa radios. Nearby nodes can relay messages across a mesh without cellular service or internet access for the radio link. Read the official introduction →

What do I need to get started?

Choose a Meshtastic-supported device, attach its correct antenna before powering it on, and use the Meshtastic app or a computer to configure it. Device setup varies by model, so follow the instructions for your exact hardware. See our getting-started guide →

What does “LongFast · US Slot 20” mean?

LongFast is a radio preset. In the US region, the default LongFast public setup uses frequency slot 20, centered at 906.875 MHz. Slot 20 is a radio frequency slot; it is not Meshtastic’s primary channel number, which is channel 0. Local groups may use different settings, so check before changing your node. See the official local-mesh guide →

Why can’t my node hear nearby nodes?

Nodes need compatible region, modem preset, frequency slot, and channel settings. They also need to be within radio reach, directly or through other relaying nodes. Check the antenna connection and compare your settings with the local group. Review node settings →

How far will a Meshtastic node reach?

There is no single reliable range number. Distance depends on terrain, buildings, antenna placement, height, interference, and whether other nodes can relay traffic. A clear line of sight and well-placed nodes can help, but a map or advertised maximum is not a guarantee for your location.

Is the default LongFast channel private?

No. The default primary channel uses a publicly known key, so anyone with compatible settings can read its messages. Treat it as public, and avoid sending sensitive information. Location sharing may also reveal where your node is. A private channel needs a different key shared only with its members. Read Meshtastic’s encryption guidance →

Does Meshtastic work without internet?

Node-to-node LoRa messaging does not require internet. Your phone or computer connects to its nearby radio, often over Bluetooth or USB. Optional internet-connected services can bridge traffic beyond the radio mesh, but they are not required for local radio communication.

Where can I ask about Maryland Mesh?

Use the Contact menu to send a question, report an issue, or suggest an update. For radio settings, include your device model and the settings shown in the app; do not include private channel links or keys.

Settings can change: the quick-look reflects the current Maryland Mesh starting recommendation. Confirm local settings before changing your node, and check official Meshtastic documentation for firmware and device-specific instructions.
